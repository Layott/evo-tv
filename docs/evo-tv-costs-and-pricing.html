2. The one cost that grows with the audience

Servers do not get more expensive because more people watch. Delivering the video does. Every viewer pulls a stream of data for as long as they watch, and that is charged by the gigabyte.

3. The real costs: licence and people

The content licence is about ₦270 million. The team is around 30 people, with the lowest paid ₦500,000 a month and about 10% of the team earning up to ₦3 million. That works out at roughly ₦22.5 million a month, which is ₦270 million a year. So in round numbers: one licence, and one year of salaries, cost about the same.

Put next to $51 a month of servers, the point is blunt: this business is a content and payroll business, not a hosting business. The technology bill only becomes interesting if you reach hundreds of thousands of viewer-hours, and by then it is a rounding error against the licence.

6. What to charge, and why

Your current ladder is ₦1,500 Supporter, ₦4,500 Premium, ₦12,000 Pro. Here is the honest read on each.

Supporter at ₦1,500

This is priced like a tip, and that is fine, but it has to cost you almost nothing to serve. Right now it removes feed ads, which means every Supporter reduces ad income as well as adding subscription income. At ₦1,500 with 6 hours of watching, the bandwidth is small, but the lost ad revenue is not automatically smaller. Keep it, but make it about status and access rather than removing income: badge, early access, the schedule ahead of time, a supporters-only chat.

Premium at ₦4,500

This is the one to build the business on, and it is priced about right for Nigeria today. For comparison, Showmax Entertainment is around ₦3,500 and its Premier League tier is higher; Netflix Nigeria mobile sits near ₦2,900 with standard plans well above that. Sitting at ₦4,500 with live sport and 1080p is defensible, but only if the catalogue justifies it every month.

If you want more subscribers rather than more per subscriber, ₦3,500 is the number that reads as "cheaper than the obvious alternative". The calculator will show you the trade: a 22% price cut needs about a 28% lift in subscribers to stand still.

Pro at ₦12,000

At eight times Supporter, this only works if it is aimed at a different kind of buyer, not a keener viewer. Creator analytics and priority ingest are business features, so sell it as a business product: a creator or a small esports org, invoiced, with a name on it. If it stays a "more premium Premium" it will sell in single digits and cost you a tier of complexity for nothing.

Annual plans

Offer ten months for the price of twelve (about a 17% discount) on Premium. It is the single cheapest way to reduce churn, and it moves cash forward, which matters when a licence has already been paid.

7. The kinds of paywall you can run

Subscription

Pay monthly, watch everything in the tier. Predictable income, and the model everything else is measured against.

Best for the main catalogue₦3,500 to ₦4,500

Rental, per title

Pay once, watch within 48 hours. Good for a single big fight or film that non-subscribers will pay for.

₦800 to ₦2,000 a title

Buy to keep

Pay once, watch whenever. Only worth it for content people rewatch, which is rare in sport and common in film.

₦2,500 to ₦6,000

Event pass

One price for one event, live plus replay for a week. This is the model that suits a league final or a big tournament.

₦1,500 to ₦5,000

Day pass

24 hours of everything. Converts people who will not commit monthly, and is the natural upsell on a big match day.

₦500 to ₦1,000

Free with ads

No payment, income from advertising. Needs scale to matter, and every subscriber you gain removes some of it.

Keep as the front door

The combination that fits EVO TV: free with ads as the front door, Premium as the subscription that carries the business, event passes on the two or three moments a year that people will pay for on their own, and Pro sold to organisations rather than to fans. Rental and buy-to-keep are only worth building once there is a film catalogue that justifies them.

8. The three things that decide whether this works

  1. Conversion, not signups. Every extra person who signs up and never pays costs you bandwidth and earns you a fraction of an advert. Moving 3% to 5% is worth more than doubling registrations.
  2. Hours watched per paying member. A ₦4,500 member who watches 40 hours a month at 1080p is close to unprofitable on bandwidth alone. Watch that number: if it climbs, the 1080p gate is what protects you.
  3. The licence is the clock. ₦270m spread over three years is ₦7.5m a month before a single salary. Everything above is about how quickly subscriptions reach that.

Server, database, storage and domain costs are the real bill for the platform as it runs today. The bandwidth ladder figures were measured on the live broadcast on 18 August 2026. Everything else is an assumption on a slider, which is the honest way to present a forecast.
